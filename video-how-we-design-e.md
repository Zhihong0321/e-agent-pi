# How we designed e — video director's brief

**Brand:** e (by Eternalgy)

**Format:** HTML and animated application UI, rendered as video

**Length:** ~73 seconds (opening: exactly 3 seconds; remaining cues align with recorded voice)

**Master:** 1920 × 1080, 16:9, 30 fps

**Updated:** 5 October 2026

## Direction

Lead with the user's requested flow:

**Document → Data → Operation → Today's separated tools and people → One shared point → e and its AI agent team.**

The first three seconds show exactly one word per second. Then show how companies manage documents today: third-party systems, separate tools, cloud and offline files, and multiple people handling connected work. Only after that problem is clear introduce the invitation: **Imagine bringing all that document work into one place.**

Keep the same information from the previous brief: documents carry operational instructions; an invoice connects customer, items, delivery, and payment; Finance, Admin, and Procurement have distinct responsibilities; e connects documents, records, and specialist agents. Reorder the explanation rather than removing it.

This is one continuous visual progression, not disconnected slides. Generic tool windows gather into e's shared workspace. Once the workspace is established, open its invoice to explain the connected data, then show a specialist handoff and close on the brand.

Use the current application's visual language and the existing film architecture. Preserve the official e logo lockup, local fonts, sample fixture, review states, and technical implementation notes below. All displayed work is controlled sample data.

## Visual language

- Use the current `/demo` palette: pale surfaces, dark readable text, green accents, rounded panels, chat, tables, and status badges.
- Keep local Inter Tight and the existing e logo lockup. Use initials and department text badges for people; no stock photography or third-party logos.
- At 1080p, use 64–96 px headings, 34–44 px supporting labels, and 26–32 px focal UI text. Enlarge the relevant panel rather than showing a tiny entire desktop.
- Keep essential content at least 96 px from frame edges, with space for optional subtitles.
- Show fragmentation through distance and boundaries, not chaotic piles or blaming employees.
- Preserve human responsibilities when the systems come together. One platform and one AI agent team do not mean one person doing every job.
- Use fades, modest translations, restrained camera moves, and connector drawing. Avoid bouncing status badges and continuous decorative motion.
- Retain draft, review, and approval boundaries. Show Paid only after its matching payment record.

## Building the animation in HTML

### Stage and camera

- **Stage Resolution:** Fixed 1920 × 1080 canvas with `overflow: hidden`.
- **Camera Scaling:** Use the proven camera helper `EF.cam(wx, wy, ww)` on `#app`. The app is designed at 1180 × 664 natural pixels; wide shots scale to `1920 / 1180` (~1.62×), while focal shots crop into chat and side panels at `~2.1×` to guarantee that 12 px text renders at 26–30 px on 1080p.
- **Coordinate Integrity:** Use the existing `EF.rel` helper only after confirming its coordinate contract. Measure connectors after font loading and convert bounds into the connector layer's coordinate space; transformed viewport bounds must account for camera scale and translation.
- **GSAP Configuration:** Execute `gsap.config({ force3D: false })` before assembling the timeline to prevent browser subpixel font blurring on scaled typography.

### One deterministic timeline

Every visible element is driven by the master timeline clock `t(r) = S + r`. Seeking to any second or frame must produce identical visual output whether jumping, rewinding, or rendering forward:

```text
renderAt(timestamp)
  → determine active scene and elapsed beat
  → derive exact document and window states
  → set connector strokeDashoffset
  → apply camera transform (EF.cam)
  → evaluate text typing / caret visibility
```

- No `Date.now()`, `Math.random()`, or uncontrolled `setTimeout()`.
- Avoid default `tl.fromTo` without `immediateRender: false` on previously tweened properties.
- Use `tl.set` for character typing and discrete state flips.

### Motion cues

| Element | Motion Treatment | Duration | Easing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening statement reveal | Opacity 0 → 1, translateY 14 → 0 px | 450 ms | `power2.out` |
| Document card expand | Expand width/height from word bounds | 550 ms | `power2.inOut` |
| SVG connector draw | `strokeDashoffset` 1 → 0 | 450–600 ms | `power1.inOut` |
| Badge status swap | Crossfade old/new status with a restrained scale change | 350 ms | `power2.out` |
| Window slide entrance | Opacity 0 → 1, translateX 20 → 0 px | 350 ms | `power2.out` |
| Unification into app | Windows dissolve, cards translate to `#app` slot | 800 ms | `power2.inOut` |
| Composer prompt typing | Character-by-character reveal with blinking caret | ~35 chars/s | linear |
| Final logo return | Scale 0.5 → 1.0, translate to center (960, 540) | 900 ms | `power2.out` |

### Render preparation

- Load local `Inter Tight` font weights (400, 700, 800) and `assets/e-logo.png` prior to resolving `EF.ready`.
- Register the timeline via `window.EF.ready.then(built => window.__timelines["e-design"] = built.timeline)`.
- Use single-worker low-memory rendering (`--workers 1 --low-memory-mode`) to preserve system resources on the local machine.
